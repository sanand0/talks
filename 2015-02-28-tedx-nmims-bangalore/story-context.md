## What might make this a great story

**Strongest candidate framing:** "The ballot was a protest." The primary intellectual move is **numbers → question → visualization → apparent anomaly → underlying human action**. This is a detective story about democracy, not an explanation of chart types.

### The cold open

Imagine receiving a ballot booklet with **1,033 names** in the 1996 Modakurichi Assembly election. Before disclosing motive, show the inconvenience of navigating it. Ask: "*How could one constituency produce more than a thousand candidates?*" The big twist comes later: a farmers' campaign used mass candidacies to demand attention; **the apparently broken election was itself the message**.

Keep the election facts specific: Modakurichi is a Tamil Nadu Assembly constituency, 1996; *not* a parliamentary seat, not a current election. The Election Commission itself confirms **1,033 candidates** and a **booklet** ballot. Retrospective journalism records **88 zero-vote candidates** and the farmers' organizing; check separate notes.

### Before any clever graphic, the messy source

At 00:17 Anand explains data from Election Commission PDFs, inconsistent spelling, and ambiguities between similar constituency names: "Madhira", "Devarakonda", "Sulurpet", etc. "Chennur" and "Chinnur" may denote **different places**. The lesson is subtler than "standardize spelling": **merging different places can be worse than failing to merge variants**.

Possible compact interactive illustration: two near-identical strings that should remain separate, and two variants that should join. If fabricated examples are necessary, mark them "illustrative"; use exact transcript examples where possible. No claim that the old dataset was fully accurate or exhaustive simply because it was cleaned.

### Represent scale — but whose scale?

At 00:53 Gramener maps candidates' assets as colored boxes in a treemap for Kannada newspaper Vijay Karnataka. One outsized box is Priya Krishna. At 01:40–03:07 Anand tries to turn crores into physical stacks of rupee notes, moving through briefcase, bed and Vidhana Soudha entrance before realizing the original huge-building metaphor doesn't work.

**Interesting storytelling twist:** the visualization briefly *fails* its own editorial test: the actual physical cash occupies less space than the imagined dramatic image. That makes the story about choosing an honest, intuitive scale, not just making a large number larger.

**Critical factual caveat:** Anand says about **₹767 crore** in a 2013 illustration; the MyNeta/ADR 2013 affidavit for Priya Krishna says about **₹911 crore declared assets**, whereas **₹770 crore** is closer to the **2009 by-election affidavit**. The 2013 media also reported ~₹910 crore. This may reflect an older graphic or figure being reused; **don't invent an explanation**. Keep Anand's recollection in quotation if relevant, annotate gently with the 2013 source, or avoid pinning the story on the disputed precise number. These are *assets*, not banknotes, and *gross assets*, not net wealth after liabilities.

At 03:07 he cites **~₹7,700 crore** for Nandan Nilekani in the 2014 Lok Sabha election. A March 2014 NDTV contemporaneous report confirms the declared-assets scale; distinguish Anand's "could buy the election" as hyperbole, not a factual or appropriate political claim.

### The screen that answered questions rather than presenting answers

At 04:57 a deeper pivot: if visuals help Gramener find stories, can a TV presenter **use the data live** when questions arrive? At 05:22 Anand describes a **98-inch touchscreen** in CNN-IBN's newsroom; Bhupendra Chaubey uses it. The funniest personal character moment is Anand behind the screen in a red T-shirt, calling home to ask family to turn on CNN-IBN.

The invention to illustrate: instead of a scripted anchor showing one result, an anchor **becomes an analyst** with a giant explorable question engine. Show an original video or slide still of the touchscreen if the video permits; don't pretend to have an authentic photograph if absent.

Brief newsroom discoveries:
- **05:46:** winner margins of only **nine votes**, in 1998 and 1999 according to talk. The hypothetical effect of family votes is a joke, not a verified counterfactual.
- **06:10:** no uncontested parliamentary contests *in the analyzed period since 1989*; there had been some earlier. Do not generalize to all elections or to after 2015 without new data.
- **06:36:** two elections with only women candidates, and only eight where women outnumbered men (scope/time period as described in talk, not independently corroborated here). May be surprising but does not need a graphic.
- **06:55:** a 99-year-old candidate (uncorroborated here). A little comic aside if included.

These are texture, not six equally sized chapters. The emotional point is that **live exploration lets the audience ask the next question**.

### False first impressions

**03:46–04:57 — "criminal cases" chart.** Anand says AAP is unexpectedly high because Udayakumar and Pushparayan each had hundreds of cases; the figures **382** and **380** match ADR's contemporaneous affidavit analysis for the 2014 election. Both were active in anti-nuclear protests in Tamil Nadu. It's a potent example of how **counts without denominators, classification, context and reasons can invite the wrong conclusion**. Do not imply:
- the presence of cases proves criminal wrongdoing;
- all 382/380 were protest-related or minor;
- a party-wide rate/share is established by these two people alone;
- activism makes every allegation irrelevant.

Safer wording: "In the 2014 declarations, two anti-nuclear activists standing for AAP had unusually large numbers of declared criminal cases. The graph counted filings; it could not adjudicate culpability." Use a link to ADR/MyNeta and distinguish reported offenses from convictions.

**07:16–08:35 — Doordarshi Party.** 1984: 97 candidates, **zero seats**. 1989: **298** according to the 2014 Economic Times / Gramener election graphic, **zero seats**. 1991: **321**, **zero seats**. Sum = **716 candidacies**, zero Lok Sabha victories. The talk says the second-largest party by *candidates fielded* in 1989, not second-largest by votes or seats. The Wikipedia table gives **288** for 1989, which conflicts with the contemporaneous Gramener graphic; document the difference or verify from original ECI files before independently asserting an exact total. Anand's colorful claim about candidates being bachelors/celibate is **not corroborated** here; reliable descriptions support teetotal vegetarian criteria. Keep the joke as attributed anecdote if at all.

**09:00–09:40 — Faridabad bellwether.** Anand says that from the constituency's creation through the 2014 Lok Sabha election, Faridabad always elected the national winner's party. That's an **as-of-2014 observation in the analyzed historical sample**, not evidence that its voters cause or predict national outcomes. "Only one such constituency" depends on exact definitions, year range and boundary changes. Avoid turning coincidence into a guaranteed predictive strategy.

Powerful visual: start with ordinary circles, let candidate-count scale balloon, **cut to the actual ballot booklet** and delayed surprise: each candidate was *part of a deliberate collective tactic*. The later 2021 and 2026 accounts from farmers provide firsthand political motives. **Don't falsely claim 88 zero votes proves nobody could find their own name**: the speaker jokes about that, but it's speculation. Some 2021/2026 reports disagree on booklet page count (50 vs 65); state "booklet" without an exact page count unless an authoritative original source settles it.

**Why the 1996 number mattered beyond spectacle:** farmers highlighted agricultural grievances; election administrators had to handle the unusual ballot. Some retrospective accounts credit the episode with later procedural/security-deposit reforms, but **causation and precise timing need care**. ECI's own FAQ documents the earlier and later deposit amounts, with a FAQ date claim that may reflect a later update; don't casually state "the election immediately caused this law."

### The risky coda

At **12:20** Anand discusses 2014 Mahasamund "Chandu Sahu" namesakes around sitting BJP MP Chandulal Sahu. At **12:47** he notices a party-wise pattern in a graphic and explicitly says, **"I'm not making any conclusions from it, just saying ... it's just an observation."**

This ending can support **the discipline of not turning a suggestive pattern into an accusation**:
- 2014 reports show **11 similarly named Chandu Sahus**, but not 11 candidates with the full **identical** legal name "Chandulal Sahu". A mix of Chandu, Chanduram, etc. appeared in reporting.
- Some coverage alleged political staging; those are **allegations**, not proven findings. Congress candidate Ajit Jogi denied knowledge in contemporary reporting.
- The presenter observes patterns; neither the transcript nor the visual alone establishes intent or who arranged entries.
- The original speaker stops here. An editorial synthesis about evidence, empathy or verification is okay, clearly demarcated as reflection.

## The narrative choice

A workable causal arc, **not mandatory table-of-contents**:
1. Open with the physically ridiculous ballot. "One constituency. A thousand candidates. Why?"
2. Backtrack: a programmer/analyst tries to answer a public-interest question, first battling disordered PDFs, then trying to make a crore visible. The first surprise is the work required to construct trustworthy inputs and scales.
3. CNN-IBN turns results into an exploratory interview with the data. Amazing outliers appear — some funny, some ethically consequential. Show why knowing **who made the data and why** matters.
4. Return to 1996: the impossible field was the tactic of unheard farmers. The data wasn't broken; **the question was too shallow**. The 2014 lookalike-names coda warns against confusing clue with verdict.

This gives the page a much sharper conclusion than "data visualizations are useful": **an anomaly is an invitation to investigate a human system**.

## Visual and editorial priorities

1. The **original 2015 slide graphics** have documentary value. If the PPTX is accessible, extract legible images/stills and add small optimized local derivatives rather than rebuilding fake graphics from memory. Attribute the talk/Gramener; take care with external press images.
2. The **2015 stage and newsroom aesthetics** can inform typography and pacing, but avoid clumsy "retro" gimmicks. The article is for 2026 readers encountering 2015.
3. One **reader prediction** before 1,033; one **data-meaning reversal** (declared criminal cases, or similar names); one **full-width original map or ballot** may be more impactful than ten cards.
4. Distinct design for the physical scale of money, the intellectual scale of the newsroom, and the human scale of farmer protest.
5. Accessible static answers exist even without JavaScript; no fabrication of official election ballots.
6. The full story page must work with **only the original published YouTube video**; seekable timestamps must align with that playback. Use the YouTube IFrame Player API for one persistent player, including the story/video toggle.
7. End with a question that changes the reader's habit: **"What human decision could explain the strangest row in your spreadsheet?"** This is *editorial*, not a recorded 2015 quote.

## Fact-check checklist

- **Confirmed:** 1996 Modakurichi had 1,033 candidates and booklet ballot (ECI).
- **Retrospective corroboration:** 88 zero votes, coordinated farmers' protest (2016, 2021, 2026 journalistic reports).
- **Confirmed contemporary sources:** candidate declarations with 382 and 380 cases (ADR); 2013 Priya Krishna ~₹911 crore (ADR/MyNeta); Nandan Nilekani ~₹7,700 crore in 2014 (NDTV); Doordarshi 97/298/321 in contemporary Gramener/ET graphic.
- **Conflicting:** Priya Krishna's 767 vs 2013 911 (possibly earlier figure), 1989 Doordarshi 298 vs Wikipedia 288, ballot-booklet pages 50 vs 65.
- **Not independently audited:** nine-vote-margin identities, 99-year-old candidacy, women-only contests, Faridabad "only one" uniqueness, precise 1984/1991 runner-up statuses, 90/250 intermediate Tamil Nadu counts, parties "affected" by namesakes.
- **Never convert:** declared cases → convictions; assets → cash; correlation → partisan culpability; historical observation → current prediction; joke → verified explanation.
