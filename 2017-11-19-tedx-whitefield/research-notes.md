# Research notes — TEDx Whitefield, 2017

**Scope:** Fact-check shelf for a later story. Sources below support mechanisms or raise alternatives; they do **not** independently reproduce Anand's archived Karnataka/Tamil Nadu/NCERT datasets. Screen-reader-friendly, on-page caveats are more useful than burying them in bibliography.

## Evidence ledger

| Talk or slide claim | What is actually supported here | Publishing instruction |

| First initial 'A' scored slightly more (00:46–01:54; slide 3) | Original slide compares Karnataka State Board exam means in **2005 and 2011**, about 8–8.5 lakh pupils/year, with letter-group averages. No causal experiment. | 'In Anand's analysis', specify years; don't say first initial *improves* exam performance. |

| Duplicate-name 2014 candidates imply orchestration (05:01–05:58; slide 8) | Counts appear in original slide, but cause and political responsibility are unverified here. | Treat as talk allegation/hypothesis, don't accuse any political party. |

Distribution shapes by themselves don't tell why, and 100 is a hard upper score bound. | Present moderation and ceiling as hypotheses, **not proof of teacher intent/genetic ability**. |
| Girls outperform boys by subject (10:58–11:49) | Talk presents historical grade averages, not controlled effects or all-India trends. | Specify subject, cohort and source; avoid biological or universal conclusions. |

| June-born students trail September-born by ~120/1200 (14:09–15:47; slide 22) | Slide cites **20 lakh Tamil Nadu Class XII students / 3 years**, but raw data/cutoff identification not rechecked here. | Attribute historical reported association; do not call 120 marks a measured causal 'birthday effect'. |
| Tamil Nadu school cutoff drives difference (15:23; slide 22) | Broader research supports relative-age effects, but cutoff schedules, cohort definitions and changing school rules require dated primary verification. | State as *plausible proposed mechanism* pending proof; never infer astrology. |

| Parents' education/occupation are strongest (16:56–17:56; slides 26–27) | NCERT National Achievement Survey Class 8, 2014 is named in slide. No individual causal coefficients/dataset rerun verified. | Strong correlates as reported, not immutable child's destiny or sole explanatory factors. |

## Independent reading

- **Relative-age effects:** systematic review, 21 articles from 24 countries: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34501556/ . The average effect for relatively younger classmates is plausible, but not a confirmation of Anand's exact Tamil Nadu month-by-month figures.
- **Separating school-starting age mechanisms:** AEA Journal of Economic Literature, 'The Economics of Age at School Entry: Insights from Evidence and Methods' (forthcoming as indexed October 2026), https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jel.20261809 . Starting age, age at testing, time in school and age relative to peers are distinct and often bundled. This is **later** context, not something known in 2017.
- **Author's retrospective framing (Apr 7, 2026):** https://www.s-anand.net/blog/speaking-unprepared/ . Anand says TEDx Whitefield 'Prisoners of Birth' assembled disparate earlier analyses of name, gender, lineage, place and time; he **did not run new analysis for that event**. This is helpful provenance: don't present the talk as a new 2017 controlled study.
- **Original slide evidence:** https://github.com/sanand0/talks/releases/download/talks/2017-11-19-TEDx-Whitefield-Prisoners-of-Birth.pptx . Text inspected from all 28 slides. Image/graph pixel and axis checks remain for the later story.
- **Older election data context:** https://talks.s-anand.net/2015-02-28-tedx-nmims-bangalore/ — the 2015 NMIMS talk elaborates the Modakurichi ballot and name-confusion story, and distinguishes allegations from verified election records. Don't mistake that for a new finding in 2017.

## Additional checks before public chart reconstruction

Get actual raw board-exam data and NCERT NAS schema/metadata, sample sizes and distributions **by year and recorded age**, definitions of 'name' (first, last or token), number of exam takers per group, missingness, board maximum and pass criteria, precise age-at-admission legislation over time, and whether the comparisons condition on income, district, school, attendance and retention. Test plausible alternative mechanisms before claiming causation.

**Unverified:** whether transcripts of the two YouTube uploads start at the same frame, historical school-entry cutoff timing, the precise source of each American birth-calendar statistic, mathematical heritability claims, and the slide's electoral motive allegations. These should remain honest gaps until checked.
