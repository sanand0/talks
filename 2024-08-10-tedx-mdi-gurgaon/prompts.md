## Find the STORY before selecting a layout

**Suggested core line:** **"The computers answered the personality questions like people. The speaker responded by changing his career."**

A compelling **cold open** is Llama 3 8B at **11:25**, calmly explaining that it gets stressed but is working on managing the stress. A *computer*. The line is funny and strange, and a reader will naturally ask, *"Wait. How did you even come to ask a computer about its feelings?"* Then rewind:

- **00:04–01:49 — Three old science-fiction dreams.** Douglas Adams's **42** is the answer to life, the universe and everything. Isaac Asimov supplies Anand's desired careers: **Hari Seldon the psychohistorian, Susan Calvin the robopsychologist, and an alien sociologist**. Reality made him an engineer and manager. Keep this personal irony alive: *science fiction set up a career that did not yet exist*.
- **01:49–04:30 — The mystery arrives.** Simon Willison retells **Alex Komoroske's alien USB-stick thought experiment**. We know the computer's next-token mechanics and can observe probability alternatives in an **llmviz** demo, but that alone doesn't predict all its quirks. Explain next-token sampling/temperature clearly without treating the metaphor as literally "think harder" or implying the demo answers the original Olympic history question reliably.
- **04:30–07:18 — An absurdly simple psychological test.** "Pick a random number from 0 to 100. Write ONLY the number NOTHING ELSE." Why require exact output? One model insisted on a friendly sentence. The audience guesses **37** for people (Veritasium). Repetition turns anecdotes into distributions: **GPT-3.5 Turbo favors 47** in the talk's example.
- **07:18–10:13 — 42 returns, but the models disagree.** **Claude 3 Haiku favors 42**, after *Hitchhiker's* planted 42 in the opening; **Gemini 1.0 Pro favors 72**. They have relatively few round numbers, single digits and repeated digits. Model choices look *humanly random-looking*, not uniform. This is the first reveal. **Do not invent a specific causal training explanation** for Douglas Adams or claim all models favor the same number. Slide **08.webp** contains 2024 recorded percentages, with ranges/temperature nuances in research-notes.md.
- **10:13–15:43 — Then they take a human personality test.** 50 Big Five / OCEAN self-description statements intended for people, including "I am the life of the party" and "I get stressed easily." GPT-4o is confident and calm; **Llama 3 8B speaks of stress and forgetfulness**; slide-based profiles portray **GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini Flash and Llama 3 8B** differently. These are **answers under the experimental 2024 prompts**, not enduring model identities. Use the very mismatch between a self-report tool and a machine as a reason to be *curious and rigorous*, not dismissive. Avoid attributing clinical traits to living celebrities from a metaphorical slide.
- **15:43–16:15 — A scientist names the job.** Andrej Karpathy used the term **"LLM psychologist"** in an early-2023 thread about the nontrivial art of prompts. That crystallizes Anand's childhood fascination. Source and dates in research-notes.md. Don't claim Karpathy has a universally verified invention priority.
- **16:15–end — The human changes identity.** Anand says he called the head of HR the previous day and got approval that morning. He announces **"LLM Psychologist"** from the stage. This is the human payoff, not evidence the computer had an inner life. His **17 Aug 2024** blog freely admits he planned the applause-generating gimmick. Whether he literally updated LinkedIn *live* needs video verification; **"world's first and only" is a self-conscious joke/claim, not an independently established world record.** Close on his 2024 triad: **Accept / Study / Use**. The reader should leave with a thought experiment they can genuinely test, not an unsupported statement about machine consciousness.

## Claims, provenance and historical discipline
