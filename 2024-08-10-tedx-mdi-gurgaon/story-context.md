## The story worth telling

**One sentence:** A science-fiction fan who wanted to understand alien minds finally finds one in a computer — first discovers its peculiar number preferences, then makes it take a personality test, and ends by changing his own job title to **LLM Psychologist**.

**Central tension:** We can inspect the software's probabilities, but that is not the same as *knowing how it will behave*. Asking models for random numbers reveals surprisingly strong, model-specific habits. Asking the same models to describe their own personalities produces hilariously human-sounding answers — which are evidence of language behavior, *not* proof of emotions or self-awareness.

**A strong cold open, at 11:25:** Meta's Llama 3 8B tells a personality questionnaire, "I can get stressed, but I'm working on managing my stress levels." A machine offering to manage its stress is funny and unsettling. How did an engineer end up interviewing computers about their emotional health? Rewind to a young Anand's science-fiction ambitions; let the running number **42** become a recurring visual/narrative motif, and return to the existentially comic career announcement at 16:15. This has a satisfying reveal: **the computers did not get a new job; their interviewer did.**

**Candidate headline:** "The Computer Says It Gets Stressed." Subtitle: "A science-fiction fan, the number 42, and the day a TEDx speaker became an LLM psychologist." Alternatives: "So I Became an Alien Psychologist"; "It Picked 42. I Changed Careers."; "How to Interview an Alien Intelligence." The main page may pick a better headline. **Do not rename the talks-catalog title 'LLM Psychology'** to match it.

The conclusion should preserve a productive ambiguity: **Treating LLMs as psychological research subjects is a useful *method* for finding behavioral regularities, not a settled claim that they possess human inner lives.** "Poke them with a stick" becomes a responsible research program: run repeatable probes, change prompts and temperatures, compare models, log surprises, verify behavior on tasks. The 2024 talk's closing call is **Accept / Study / Use** — no generic AI evangelism or 2026 agentic-AI manifesto.

## The discovery sequence — exact talk anchors, flexible story order

### Opening paradox: the computer tells us it gets stressed (11:25–12:30)

At **11:25**, while reviewing responses to 50 Big Five questions, Anand quotes Llama 3 8B describing itself as forgetful and stressed, "working on managing my stress levels". This is delightful because it confuses the human setting (stress / forgetfulness / belongings / socializing) with software language. Let the reader hear this brief moment, then ask: *How does a machine get stressed?* Do not answer "because it has emotions"; a model's first-person text can be an imitation, a result of tuning, or other context-driven behavior.

### Rewind: three science-fiction job applications (00:04–02:46)

- **00:04–00:53:** Douglas Adams's Deep Thought spends "seventy-five thousand generations" and proclaims **42** as the answer to life, the universe and everything. The number is the comic's recurring puzzle piece and resurfaces in Anthropic's random-number outputs around **07:18**. Do not quote substantial copyrighted dialogue from the Adams/TV excerpt; summarize the setup or show "42".
- **00:53–01:49:** Isaac Asimov's **Hari Seldon / psychohistory, Susan Calvin / robopsychology, alien sociology in The Gods Themselves** shaped Anand's career fantasies. *He became an engineer and manager instead.* This unfulfilled personal ambition is an essential seed for the final twist. Slide **03.webp** contains these roles.
- **01:49–02:46:** A strange gift: **Simon Willison's USB-stick aliens**. Willison attributed the metaphor to **Alex Komoroske** in his 3 Aug 2023 talk; don't claim Simon originated it. Their intelligences' behavior is only partly documented, so experiment with them. Slide **05.webp**. Link https://simonwillison.net/2023/Aug/3/weird-world-of-llms/ .

### What happens inside the mysterious USB? (02:46–04:30)

The live **llmviz** demo shows next-token probability alternatives after a prompt about the first Olympic gold medal. At **03:53**, Anand describes choosing an 11%-probability "event" over other tokens and introduces **temperature**. Slide **05a.webp** and https://sanand0.github.io/llmviz/ . The output is not a history reference about Olympic medals; it's a worked demonstration of token prediction. Even this simplified "choice among next tokens" only partly explains system behavior: decoding isn't literally "thinking harder" at greater temperature, and probability display is not direct access to inner motivations. Offer a tiny schematic branch that illustrates probabilities and temperature if it improves understanding; mark any simulation as illustrative, not recorded API output.

### The alien has favorite numbers (04:30–10:13)

- **04:30–05:46:** Ask Llama for a random integer **0–100**; it answers 53 *plus a helpful explanation*. Re-prompt with "**Write ONLY the number NOTHING ELSE**". Audience guesses **37** when asked which number humans favor; Anand invokes the **March 2024 Veritasium video** https://youtu.be/d6iQrh2TK98 .
- **05:46–06:33:** Live examples include 47. The meaning is repeatable data, not one anecdotal answer. Important: 2024 results are archived in https://github.com/sanand0/llmrandom and local ~/code/llmrandom/llmrandom.json and results.json under **legacy-2024** runs. Original design used **200 requests per temperature condition**, temperatures 0.0–1.0, on GPT-3.5 Turbo, Claude 3 Haiku and Gemini 1.0 Pro; the newer 2026 runs coexist in results.json and **must not be mixed into the 2024 chart**.
- **06:33–08:18:** The 2024 favorites as spoken: **GPT-3.5 Turbo ~47**, **Claude 3 Haiku ~42**, **Gemini Pro ~72**, with many outputs ending in 7 and rare round/single-digit/repeated-digit choices. The number **42**, planted by Deep Thought, returns unexpectedly. **07a.webp** shows the chart; **08.webp** summarizes category frequencies. Never imply all models prefer 42.
- **08:18–09:12:** Temperature affects the sampling distribution, but does not guarantee uniform random sampling. Show how an ostensibly objective computer picks "random-looking" numbers with human-language flavor; do not assert this proves human-like thinking or that Douglas Adams specifically *caused* 42 in Claude's training.
- **09:12:** Slide **08.webp** contains measured rates (%) for **GPT / Gemini / Claude**, respectively: **multiples of ten 0.3 / 0.3 / 0; single-digit 0 / 0.7 / 0; repeated digits 1.5 / 4.0 / 0.2**. These are old, run-specific chart values; source raw results/conditions and denominator if reproducing. Uniform benchmark for integers 0..100 inclusive: 11/101 multiples of 10 (including 0 and 100), 10/101 single-digit including zero, 9/101 repeated double-digit; labels in slide and narration can use different range definitions. Explain exactly what is counted.
- Best interaction idea: ask visitor **"What number would *you* choose?"** then compare their guess with **real 2024 distribution plots**, underlined by "not a new API call, not a psychological diagnosis of you." Sample selection should make the early 42 payoff even stronger.

### The personality interview (10:13–15:43)

- **10:13:** Big Five/OCEAN test, **50 self-report prompts** drawn from the public-domain IPIP item pool (https://ipip.ori.org/). E.g., "Am the life of the party", "Feel little concern for others", "Am always prepared", "Get stressed out easily", "Have a rich vocabulary". **09–11.webp** show the actual 2024 questionnaire/responses.
- **10:52:** **GPT-4o** presents itself as prepared, calm, not the life of the party. **11:25:** **Llama 3 8B** produces charmingly human "I get stressed" and "I sometimes forget" answers. Keep these as *model-written first-person quotes* from specific 2024 tests, not facts about a sentient mind.
- **12:30–14:00:** OCEAN traits explained using celebrities on the original slides **12.webp**. Those are speaker's loose *illustrations/subjective caricatures*, not validated evaluations of those public figures. Better explain the five dimensions without asserting which real humans "are" neurotic, disagreeable, etc.
- **14:00–15:43:** Four **2024-era snapshots**: **GPT-4o:** high apparent openness/agreeableness; **Claude 3.5 Sonnet:** talkative, self-described emotional; **Gemini Flash:** quiet and opinionated; **Llama 3 8B:** calm/conservative in the onstage summary. Slides **13–16.webp** show original model-specific profiles. **Careful:** ~/code/llmpersonality/script.js now contains results for **Llama 3.1 8B** and other evolving examples, *not identical* to the talk's **Llama 3 8B**. Do not copy later live-site values and present them as exact August 2024 results.
- Meaning: *Answers may be repeatable enough to compare under a given instrument, yet a human self-report instrument's scores are not automatically validated personality traits for LLMs.* Context, prompt formatting, response scale, sampling and model version matter. No silent shift from "responded as if stressed" to "experiences stress".

## Fact-check discipline

Consult research-notes.md for an evidence ledger and links.md for URLs. Specific traps:
1. **Behavior is not consciousness/personality.** The 50-item instrument assesses a model's *responses*, not feelings.
2. **The 2024 project ≠ its later updated live page.** Keep 2024 data, models, parameter settings and published slides together; put any 2026 repeat experiment clearly in an optional dated epilogue, not the 2024 narrative.
3. **"Random" is prompt-derived text, not a cryptographic RNG test.** Different parameters/ranges, formatting and models produce different distributions; report the right denominator.
4. **Do not turn reported numbers and illustrations into scientific laws.** Simon's metaphor has an earlier attribution; Karpathy's term and world's-first claim are distinct; historical scene remains 2024.
5. **Dates:** Aug 10 performance, Aug 14 institutional announcement, Aug 17 blog. The 2024 workshop/papers and later academic research have their own publication dates; explicitly label post-talk reflection.
6. **Video uniqueness:** only youtu.be/rA__c6CosVI. A differently uploaded YouTube recording exists in old project documentation, but do not add it to page/config as a backup; certainly no WebM.
7. **Copyright:** keep original slides and web-original comic, summarize rather than reproduce substantial Douglas Adams / *Hitchhiker's* text or separate audiovisual clips.
