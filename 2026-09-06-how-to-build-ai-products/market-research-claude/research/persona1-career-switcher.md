## Persona: Kenji, 34 – Osaka Marketing Coordinator

# Persona & Context

**Name:** Kenji | **Age:** 34 | **Location:** Osaka

**Job:** Marketing coordinator at mid-size company. Wants to move into tech-adjacent role.

**Goal for this app:** Learn to build websites (bought Udemy course, has YouTube bookmarks, never finished).

**Core problem:** Choice paralysis. Too many guides, stacks, courses—freezes and does nothing.

**Available time:** 20–45 minutes some weeknights; sometimes only 10–15 min when tired.

**Tech comfort:** Moderate. Excel, Slack, browsing—not a programmer.

**Attitude toward apps:** Skeptical. Burned by "productivity app" hype before; wants proof something actually works.

## Step 6: Testing Deviations

### Attempt A: "See a Smaller Step"

**Action:** Clicked "もっと小さな一歩を見る" (See a smaller step)

**What I expected:** A smaller variation of the same suggestion, shown on the same screen.

**What actually happened (initially):** Seemed to take me back to the landing page entirely, which was frustrating.

**BUT:** On closer inspection (re-reading the result screen), the "smaller step" option WAS actually shown on the result page itself in a separate section: "もっと小さくするなら / 例題一問に使うページや道具を開くだけで、今日は十分です。" → *"To make it even smaller: just opening the page/tools for one problem is enough for today."* This is actually good UX, but it's confusing because the button label doesn't clearly indicate it updates the same page.

### Attempt B: "Suggest Another Method"

**Action:** Clicked "別の方法を提案してもらう" (Suggest another method)

**What I expected:** See an alternative approach to reach the same goal, keeping my time/tools constraints.

**What actually happened:** Taken back to Q1 of the wizard, implying I need to re-answer the questions to get a different suggestion.

**In-character reaction (Kenji):**
- This is frustrating. I don't want to change my situation; I just want to see a different method at the same constraints. The app is forcing me to adjust my answers rather than offering flexibility at the suggestion stage.
- It feels like the app's way of saying "if you don't like this, re-do the quiz"—not "here's another option for you."

# What Worked Well

- **Landing page tone:** Non-judgmental, specific instruction ("don't need to be detailed"), removes shame. Makes you feel like the app designers understand procrastinators.
  
- **Example chips:** Seeing your exact goal as an option ("Build my own website") normalizes the goal and builds instant trust that you're in the right place.

- **The 3-question quiz structure:** Each question progressively narrows the suggestion. The questions are about YOUR situation, not abstract tech concepts. Feels personalized.

- **Q2 instruction:** "You don't need to pick the longest time" explicitly removes pressure about available time. Small detail, big impact for someone like Kenji.

- **"Reason for this suggestion" (理由):** The app explains WHY it picked that suggestion, not just what to do. This builds trust because you can evaluate if the logic makes sense for you. ("We're not redoing basics; we're improving one small part" = smart triage.)

- **Micro-action framing:** "Change one button" or "explain one problem line" or "open one tool"—these are genuinely small enough to do tonight without guilt. Success is achievable.

- **Local-browser storage:** No account, no data harvesting, no cloud. For a skeptical user, this is refreshing.

- **Smaller step expansion:** The "see a smaller step" feature showing an even tinier option on the same page is helpful (once you understand how it works).

# Friction / Confusion / Bugs

### 🔴 **Severity: Blocker**
**Goal Input Lost / Switched**
- **What happened:** Entered "自分のWebサイトを作る" (Build my own website), but result screen showed "簿記3級に合格する" (Bookkeeping exam).
- **Why it matters to Kenji:** He came here to solve a specific problem (web building) and the app forgot it. This is a fundamental trust failure. If the app can't remember the most basic input, why would he trust its suggestions? He'd abandon immediately.
- **Reproducibility:** Happened consistently when clicking example chips or using text input.

### 🟠 **Severity: Major**
**"Suggest Another Method" Takes You Back to Q1 Instead of Offering Alternatives**
- **What happened:** Clicked "別の方法を提案してもらう," expecting to see a different approach with the same constraints. Instead, returned to the wizard's first question.
- **Why it matters to Kenji:** He's already told the app his situation; he doesn't want to re-answer. He wants to explore options. The UX implies he has to change his answers to see alternatives, which feels like the app is dismissing his actual constraints rather than respecting them.

### 🟠 **Severity: Major**
**Unclear Navigation After "See Smaller Step"**
- **What happened:** Clicking "もっと小さな一歩を見る" seemed to go back to the landing page at first, but the smaller option was actually shown on the same result page in a separate section.
- **Why it matters to Kenji:** He'd be confused whether he succeeded or failed. Is there a smaller step, or do I have to start over? Ambiguity breaks confidence.

### 🟡 **Severity: Minor**
**Reason Text Mismatch (if goal is wrong)**
- **Example reason shown:** "This isn't redoing basics; it's improving one small part of something you already have."
- **Problem:** For a beginner like Kenji, he hasn't built anything yet, so "something you already have" doesn't apply. The suggestion assumes prior work.
- **Note:** This might be correct if the app intended "something you already have" to mean the CodePen template, but it's not explicit.

### 🟡 **Severity: Nitpick**
**Example Chip Interaction Unclear**
- **What happened:** Clicking an example chip seemed to both select the goal AND advance to Q1, or maybe it only advanced. The flow wasn't clear.
- **Why it matters to Kenji:** First-time users might not understand if they've "entered" their goal or if they still need to confirm.

# Missing Features / Suggestions

1. **Explicit Goal Confirmation Screen**
   - After entering a goal, show a screen: "Your goal: [text]. Is this right? [Yes / Edit]" before advancing to Q1. Prevents goal loss.

2. **"See Alternative Methods" Without Re-doing Wizard**
   - At the result stage, offer 2–3 alternative approaches for the SAME time/tools/situation constraints. Let the user explore without re-answering.

3. **Progress Indicator**
   - A breadcrumb or progress bar (like "Q1 > Q2 > Q3 > Suggestion") would clarify the flow, especially if users navigate backward.

4. **Save/Share Suggestions**
   - Add a "Save" or "Copy to clipboard" feature for the suggestion text so users can refer back without opening the app.

5. **Session History (Optional)**
   - Show recent goals tested, so repeat users can re-run old suggestions or see a timeline of progress. (This could be a nice "motivation" feature for retention.)

6. **Beginner-Friendly Phrasing for From-Scratch Learners**
   - When the situation is "haven't started," avoid phrasing like "improve something you already have." Acknowledge: "You're starting fresh. Here's the tiniest first step."

# Trust, Emotion & Retention

## Would Kenji Actually Do the Suggested Action Tonight?

**If the goal were correct:** Probably yes, cautiously. The 20-minute budget is realistic, and "change one button and see what happens" feels achievable. He'd open CodePen and try it.

**But the goal is wrong:** He'd close the app and tell his coworker, "It didn't work—it forgot what I was trying to learn."

## Would He Open It Again Tomorrow?

**No.** The goal-switching bug is a deal-breaker. On the second visit, he'd remember "that app that lost my goal" and wouldn't risk wasting time.

## Would He Tell His Coworker It's Worth Trying?

**Only if he somehow got past the bug.** Then he might say: *"If it works, the suggestions are actually small enough to do. But it has bugs—my goal got switched somehow. Maybe wait for a fix."* His recommendation would be qualified, not enthusiastic.

## What's Missing for Trust & Retention?

1. **Reliability:** The goal input MUST work perfectly. That's table stakes.
2. **Transparency:** When a feature (like "smaller step" or "another method") changes what's shown, it should be unmistakably clear.
3. **Respect for Constraints:** Let users explore options without re-answering. Kenji already told you his situation; don't make him repeat it.
4. **Small Wins:** The micro-action framing is great. If Kenji actually completes one 20-minute step, he might come back to find the next one. Retention hinges on delivering a few real wins.
5. **No Account Pressure:** The no-login design is smart for trust. Keep it.

---

# One-Line Verdict

Study Walker has the right insight (procrastinators need tiny, achievable steps, not grand plans) and smart UX scaffolding (3-question quiz, "reason for suggestion"), but a critical goal-input bug breaks the core promise—if the app can't remember what you're trying to learn, why trust its advice?
