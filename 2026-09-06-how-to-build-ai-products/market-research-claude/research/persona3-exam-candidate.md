3. "一か所変えた結果を、変更前と一度だけ見比べます。" (Compare your explanation with the correct answer once)

**Completion goal:** "最初の操作を試し、その結果を確認できた" (Try the first action and confirm the result)

*Positive:*

*Helpful and not generic nonsense, but lacks subject-specific depth.*

# What Worked Well

- **Landing page is honest and approachable.** "Don't worry about being detailed" removes friction. The fill-in-the-blank format is friendlier than an open text field.

- **Example chips are practical and diverse.** Includes a real exam goal ("簿記3級に合格する"), not just vague aspirations. Signals the app understands structured learning.

- **Q1 options meaningfully differentiate stuck-states.** The five options cover different failure modes (haven't started, overwhelmed by choices, tried but stalled, abandoned, unsure what's next). This is thoughtful segmentation.

- **Recommendations adapt to stuck-state.** First attempt (unsure what's next) → metacognitive approach. Second attempt (tried but difficult) → foundational approach. The app isn't giving the same advice to everyone.

- **Reasoning is transparent.** Each recommendation includes "この提案にした理由" (Why this suggestion) — the app explains its logic. This builds trust, even if the logic is generic.

- **Granularity adjustment ("smaller step") is smart UX.** Acknowledges that motivation and time availability vary within a session. Lowers activation barrier.

- **"Prepare your usual tools" messaging.** No gatekeeping behind apps, subscriptions, or special software. You can use what you already have.

- **Time estimates are realistic.** "20分で始められる大きさにしています" (sized to fit in 20 minutes) is honest. Doesn't over-promise.

# Friction / Confusion / Bugs

## Bug: "別の方法を提案してもらう" Resets Session

**What I clicked:** The "別の方法を提案してもらう" (Suggest another method) button on the result screen  
**Expected behavior:** Show a different study recommendation (alternative strategy, same goal)  
**Actual behavior:** Entire app reset to home screen; all answers and result lost  
**Severity: Major**

**Why it matters to this persona:** An exam-prep candidate wants to compare strategies ("should I do a full practice exam or analyze one wrong problem?"). Instead, the button just nuked the session. For someone with limited evening time, this is flow-breaking and frustrating. Rebuild trust required.

## Limitation: No Subject-Specific Context

**Observation:** The app gives generic study-strategy advice (metacognitive reflection, vocabulary building) that could apply to any exam.

**Gap:** For bookkeeping certification specifically, there's no mention of:
- Known difficult topics (consolidated entries, trial balances, journal entries vs. ledger)
- Common student weak points
- Different difficulty curves (foundation → intermediate → advanced topics)
- Which section of the exam is most heavily weighted

**Example:** If I said "I'm struggling," the app suggests "find unknown vocabulary." That might be right. Or I might need to re-learn debit/credit rules, or do timed practice, or watch a video. The app doesn't know.

**Why it matters to this persona:** Daisuke is pragmatic. He wants to maximize exam-pass probability per minute. Generic advice is not good enough; he needs to know "do X because it addresses your specific weakness, and it's known to move the needle on the 簿記3級."

**Severity: Major**  
*Reason: For a structured exam like a professional certification, subject-matter expertise is table-stakes. The app's generic approach feels less credible than a human tutor, textbook, or exam-prep course.*

---

## Limitation: No Adaptive Follow-Up After First Recommendation

**Observation:** After completing the first recommendation (analyze one wrong problem), there's no built-in follow-up: *Did you actually do this? What did you learn? Did it help?* The app just offers "smaller step" or "another method"—both feature the same recommendation or reset everything.

**Why it matters to this persona:** A 3-month exam prep requires momentum and feedback loops. Daisuke needs: *"Okay, you did that. Here's what to do next based on whether it helped."* Instead, the app is stateless. It doesn't know if he followed the advice, whether it worked, or how to build a study sequence.

**Severity: Nitpick** (out of scope for a micro-action recommender, but a limit to retention)

---

## Limitation: "過去問" Mention is Underdeveloped

**Observation:** In Scenario 2 (tried but difficult), the recommendation mentions "過去問を一問だけ開き" (open one past exam problem). This is actually exam-relevant. But then it just says "find one unknown word" — no guidance on *which* problems, no link to resources, no explanation of why this is the right next step for someone who's already tried and failed.

**Why it matters to this persona:** Saying "use past exams" signals the app understands exam prep. But not connecting Daisuke to actual past papers or explaining the strategy feels incomplete. *"I should use past papers. Great. Where? How many? In what order?"*

**Severity: Minor**  
*Reason: It's not a blocker, but it leaves credibility on the table.*

# Missing Features / Suggestions

1. **"Suggest another method" should actually suggest a different strategy**, not reset the session. Either:
   - Show 2–3 alternative recommendations in a carousel, or
   - Offer a new Q1 answer and regenerate, with a clear "Try a different approach" label

2. **Add a "Did this help?" follow-up screen** after the user attempts a recommendation. If yes → suggest next step. If no → offer alternatives. This builds a study sequence, not just one-off nudges.

3. **Integrate real resources or at least resource templates** for bookkeeping or other structured exams. Example:
   - "Here are past 簿記3級 exam papers: [link]"
   - "Here are the most common mistake patterns: [link]"
   - If the app can't provide these, offer a template for where the user should find them

4. **Add subject-selection at the home screen** ("What are you studying for?"). This would allow the app to:
   - Tailor language (e.g., "past exam papers" for exams vs. "tutorials" for skill-building)
   - Offer subject-aware recommendations
   - Give better follow-up guidance

5. **Show or remember study history within a session.** Let the user see:
   - "You said you're unsure about next steps. We suggested analyzing one wrong problem. Want to try a smaller version, or get a completely different idea?"
   - Current UX loses the context.

6. **For "already tried but stuck" scenario, add a failure-mode diagnosis.**  
   Example: "When you tried, what got in the way? Was it understanding the concepts, solving problems quickly, or knowing which topics to focus on?" This narrows the recommendation further.

# Trust, Emotion & Retention

## Would Daisuke come back tomorrow?

**Likelihood: 50/50, depends on whether the first recommendation worked.**

If Daisuke did the first recommendation (analyze one wrong problem) and found it genuinely helpful (e.g., *"Oh, I see why I got that wrong. I wasn't thinking about X clearly."*), he'd probably use the app again—it got him unstuck with minimal friction.

If he tried it and found it generic or unhelpful, he wouldn't come back. The app offers no learning loop or persistence; each session is isolated. Once burned, why return?

**Retention risk: High**  
*Reason: No memory, no follow-up, no feedback mechanism. The app is a one-time nudge factory, not a study companion.*

# One-line Verdict

**Study Walker is a thoughtful, lightweight study-kickstarter with good UX and basic personalization, but it lacks subject-matter depth and retention hooks—useful for a quick motivational nudge when stuck, not a credible exam-strategy guide for a pragmatic candidate 3 months from a professional certification.**

## Summary of Findings for Product Team

**Top 3 Strengths:**
1. **Smart Q1 segmentation.** The five stuck-states actually differentiate user needs, and recommendations adapt meaningfully. This is where the app shines.
2. **Granularity adjustment (smaller step feature).** Excellent UX for lowering activation barriers and respecting different motivation/time states within a session.
3. **Transparent reasoning.** Each recommendation includes "why we suggested this," which builds trust and helps users understand the app's logic—even if they disagree with it.

**Top 3 Weaknesses:**
1. **"Suggest another method" button resets the entire session instead of offering an alternative strategy.** This breaks expected UX and frustrates users mid-flow. (Bug or design flaw, either way: high priority fix.)
2. **No subject-specific guidance.** The app's recommendations are generic self-help techniques (metacognition, vocabulary building) that could apply to any learner. For structured exams like the 簿記3級, this undermines credibility. A pragmatic candidate needs to know: "Do this because it specifically addresses a known weak point in the 3級 exam."
3. **No retention loop.** Sessions are stateless. After a recommendation, the app doesn't ask "did this work?" or offer a next step. Each use is isolated, which limits coming-back behavior and sequencing of learning over time.

**For exam-focused users specifically:**  
Study Walker works as a "get unstuck with a micro-task" tool, but not as a "guide me to exam success" tool. It's more motivational app than study strategy advisor.
