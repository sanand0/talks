I have a conflict with my sister with my property. Write an official document an email where I want to give up the property to my sister. How do I write it professionally?

## Perplexity

Yes — you can write a clear, professional email stating that you wish to relinquish your interest in the property to your sister. However, an email alone does **not** legally transfer property in Singapore; you will normally need a properly executed transfer document (often a **Deed of Gift** for private property, or an HDB change-of-ownership application for an HDB flat), plus stamp duty and registration steps.

```markdown
## Professional email template

**Subject:** Formal Notice of Intention to Relinquish My Interest in \[Property Address\]

Dear \[Sister’s Name\],

I am writing to formally confirm my intention to relinquish all my right, title, and interest in the property located at **\[full property address\]**, currently held under **\[Title Deed / HDB lease / co-ownership details\]**, in your favour.

This decision is made voluntarily and without any payment, compensation, or condition from you. I wish for you to assume full ownership and responsibility for the property, including any future mortgage payments, maintenance costs, property tax, insurance, and other obligations associated with it.

I propose that we proceed with the necessary legal and administrative steps to effect the transfer, including engaging a solicitor where required, completing the relevant transfer or gift documentation, settling any applicable stamp duties or fees, and notifying the relevant authorities.

Please confirm your acceptance of this arrangement in writing. Once confirmed, I am prepared to cooperate fully to complete the transfer process.

This email is intended to record my intention and does not by itself constitute a legal transfer of ownership.

Yours sincerely,
**Anand S**
\[NRIC / identification number, if appropriate\]
\[Contact number\]
\[Date\]
```

## Important points to include

- Identify the property precisely by its full address and title/lease reference.
- State that you are giving up your interest **voluntarily and without consideration**.
- Make clear who will bear outstanding mortgage, renovation, tax, and maintenance liabilities.
- Ask for written acceptance from your sister.
- Keep the email as a record, but do not treat it as the final legal transfer.

## Singapore-specific cautions

- For an **HDB flat**, transfers to siblings are possible only where HDB accepts the reason and the proposed owner meets eligibility conditions; HDB requires the incoming owner to be at least 21 and generally a Singapore Citizen or PR.[](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/-/media/managing-my-home/home-ownership/change-of-flat-owners-or-occupiers/change-in-flat-ownership-not-through-a-sale/application-process/change-in-flat-ownership-terms-and-conditions.pdf)
- For a **private property**, a gift is usually effected through a Deed of Gift, followed by stamping and registration with the relevant authorities.[](https://singaporelegaladvice.com/law-articles/transfer-of-property-in-singapore/)
- A gift does not avoid Buyer’s Stamp Duty: BSD is generally based on the higher of the stated price or market value, and ABSD may also apply even where the transfer is a gift.
- For an HDB family transfer, Buyer’s Stamp Duty and Seller’s Stamp Duty remissions may be available only if the IRAS conditions are met, including that no consideration is paid except repayment of the outgoing owner’s CPF funds.[](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/appeals-refunds-reliefs-and-remissions/common-stamp-duty-remissions-and-reliefs-for-property/transfer-of-hdb-flats-within-family)

Because this involves a family conflict and a valuable asset, I’d recommend having a Singapore property lawyer review the final transfer documents before either of you signs anything.
